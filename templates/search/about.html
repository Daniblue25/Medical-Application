Strategic Positioning

MedSearch positions itself as the reference tool for high-precision medical and surgical research. Unlike general search engines or purely AI-based solutions, MedSearch prioritizes clinical reliability, source transparency, and workflow efficiency (search → selection → export). Version 3.2 extends its scope to nursing research with 144 dedicated journals, using a unified geographic detection method (last author affiliation) across all search types.

Competitive Analysis

1. PubMed (The Gold Standard)

Strength: Official NIH source. Total comprehensiveness (36+ million articles). Free and open-access.

Weakness: Austere 2000s interface, no "professional" filters for surgeons, laborious export (raw CSV difficult to exploit).

MedSearch Advantage: We use the same PubMed database but with a "Surgeon-First" interface. The "Rank A+" filter saves 80% of time on manual sorting.

2. Cure AI / Elicit.org (The AI Challengers)

Strength: Impressive automatic summaries via GPT-4, natural language questions, modern interface.

Weakness: Risk of hallucinations (AI can invent data), "Black Box", no massive formatted Excel export.

MedSearch Advantage: MedSearch accelerates access to verified raw data. Formatted Excel export is unique in the world for building systematic reviews.

3. Scopus / Web of Science (The Academics)

Strength: Bibliometric power (who cites whom?), multidisciplinary coverage, citation network analysis.

Weakness: Very expensive (€5,000 - €50,000 / year), complex, not focused on clinical practice.

MedSearch Advantage: Free, lightweight, fast and focused on care and surgery. Unique batch search (10 parallel terms).

What's New in v3.2 (February 2026)

A. Nursing Research Support (144 Journals)

Problem solved: No dedicated search filter for nursing literature.

Solution: New "Nurse" rank option with 144 curated nursing journals.

Gain: Uniform region detection (last author affiliation) across Surgery and Nursing searches.

B. Smart Incremental Cache (SQLite)

Problem solved: Re-running the same search re-fetched everything from PubMed.

Solution: Incremental merge — only truly new articles are fetched and added to cache. Results served instantly when fresh (< 7 days).

Gain: Response time < 100 ms for cached queries, reduced API load.

C. Batch Search (Parallel multi-query)

Problem solved: Comparing 10 terms required 10 manual searches.

Solution: Separate your terms with ; or new line. MedSearch launches up to 10 parallel queries.

Gain: 10 queries in 8 seconds (vs 30 seconds manually).

D. Optimized Excel Export

Columns: Sample Size, Study Type, Region, Outcomes, Confidence Score.

Meta-analysis ready format: Compatible with RevMan, Cochrane, PRISMA.

E. Improved Stability & Testing

76 unit tests covering extractors, region detection, and API views.

Cleaned dependencies, health check endpoint, and hardened security middleware.

Good to Know

MedSearch v3.2 is not a competitor to PubMed or Scopus. It's a productivity amplifier for surgeon-researchers and nursing researchers.
