Strategic Positioning

MedLitSearch positions itself as the reference tool for high-precision medical and surgical research. Unlike general search engines or purely AI-based solutions, MedLitSearch prioritizes clinical reliability, source transparency, and workflow efficiency (search → selection → export). Version 3.2 extends its scope to nursing research with 144 dedicated journals, using a unified geographic detection method (last author affiliation) across all search types.

Competitive Analysis

1. PubMed (The Gold Standard)

Strength: Official NIH source. Total comprehensiveness (36+ million articles). Free and open-access.

Weakness: Austere 2000s interface, no "professional" filters for surgeons, laborious export (raw CSV difficult to exploit).

MedLitSearch Advantage: We use the same PubMed database but with a "Surgeon-First" interface. The "Rank A+" filter saves 80% of time on manual sorting.

2. Cure AI / Elicit.org (The AI Challengers)

Strength: Impressive automatic summaries via GPT-4, natural language questions, modern interface.

Weakness: Risk of hallucinations (AI can invent data), "Black Box", no massive formatted Excel export.

MedLitSearch Advantage: MedLitSearch accelerates access to verified raw data. Formatted Excel export is unique in the world for building systematic reviews.

3. Scopus / Web of Science (The Academics)

Strength: Bibliometric power (who cites whom?), multidisciplinary coverage, citation network analysis.

Weakness: Very expensive (€5,000 - €50,000 / year), complex, not focused on clinical practice.

MedLitSearch Advantage: Free, lightweight, fast and focused on care and surgery. Unique batch search (10 parallel terms).

Methodology & Scoring

How extraction confidence and quality indicators work

Extraction Confidence Levels

Every extracted field (primary outcome, sample size, multicenter status, safety data) carries a confidence level produced by the regex engine. It reflects how unambiguously the information was found in the abstract, not a judgment on study quality.

High confidence

The abstract uses an explicit, standardized phrase such as "The primary outcome was…", "Primary endpoint: …" or "Overall survival was the primary endpoint." The regex matched a specific, high-specificity pattern.

Medium confidence

The abstract uses a colon-style labeling ("Outcomes: …", "Main outcome measures:") or a slightly ambiguous phrasing. The extracted text is likely correct but may need verification.

Low confidence

The extraction relied on a vague contextual pattern or an unusual phrasing. The text shown may be a related sentence, not the true primary outcome. Manual verification is recommended.

Sample Size Extraction

The engine looks for participant counts with contextual keywords such as enrolled, randomized, included, analysed. Numbers near screening-only words (screened, assessed for eligibility, identified) are deliberately excluded to avoid confusing the CONSORT funnel count with the actual study sample.

Confidence Trigger condition Example phrase
High Strong enrollment verb near a number "874 patients were randomized"
Medium Number near a weaker inclusion verb "We included 312 participants"
Low Fallback: largest plausible number in the abstract "A total of 58 cases were reviewed"

"Partial Extraction" Warning

When a study detail panel shows the banner "Partial extraction — non-standard abstract", it means the regex engine found no exploitable pattern in the abstract text.

Common reasons:

  • The author used Primary Endpoint instead of Primary Outcome — or other non-standard synonyms.
  • The abstract is unstructured (narrative style without labeled sections).
  • The article is a review, editorial, or case report that does not describe a primary outcome.
  • The abstract is in a language other than English or French.

This is a limitation of rule-based extraction, not a flaw in the study. Always consult the full text on PubMed for definitive information.

Multicenter Detection

A study is classified as multicentric when explicit keywords are found in the abstract or title (multicenter, multicentre, multi-institutional, multi-site, or explicit center counts like "across 12 hospitals"). When the evidence is weaker (e.g., multiple affiliations without an explicit keyword), the confidence is set to medium. Absence of these markers yields a monocentric classification.
