About MedLitSearch

What it does, how it works, and its limits

Overview

MedLitSearch is a web application that searches PubMed in real time through the NCBI E-utilities and extracts a few characteristics of clinical studies from their abstracts. It was developed at the DRCI of CHU Clermont-Ferrand for clinicians, researchers and research teams who screen the literature.

Extraction relies on deterministic rules (regular expressions and dictionaries). It uses no machine-learning or generative model. Three properties follow from this design:

Reproducible

The same abstract always gives the same extracted value, with the same software version.

Traceable

Extracted values come from the abstract text, where they can be checked. Bibliographic data are the raw PubMed records.

Auditable

The rules are open source. Each error can be traced back to the rule that produced it.

Limits

  • The Outcome Extractor is validated only for detecting an explicit primary outcome, not for the meaning of the extracted text.
  • The sample size is unreliable when the abstract reports several competing counts (study arms, sub-populations, registries, large observational cohorts).
  • The region is that of the last author's affiliation, not the recruitment location. Articles without a usable affiliation have no region.
  • Extraction rules are designed for abstracts in English and French only.
  • The time saved by the application has not been measured.
  • The impact factor describes the journal, not the quality of an individual study.

Extracted values support screening; they do not replace reading the article.

Methodology

How the rules work and what the confidence levels mean

Confidence levels

Each rule carries a fixed confidence level, set when the rule was written. It shows how explicit the matched wording is. It is not a judgment on the study, and it has not been validated as a probability of being correct.

High

Explicit, standard wording, such as "The primary outcome was…" or "874 patients were randomized".

Medium

Label-style or less specific wording, such as "Main outcome measures: …". Likely correct; check it.

Low

Indirect wording. The value may come from a related sentence: check it against the abstract.

Sample size

The rules look for participant counts next to words such as enrolled, randomized, included, analysed. When a screening count and an enrolment count are both reported ("312 patients were screened, of whom 120 were enrolled"), the enrolment count is kept. When several candidate numbers remain, the one found by the highest-scoring rule is kept: this is where most errors occur.

"Partial extraction" warning

The banner "Partial extraction — non-standard abstract structure" means that no rule matched the abstract.

Common reasons:

  • The author used wording that no rule covers.
  • The abstract is unstructured (narrative style without labeled sections).
  • The article is a review, editorial or case report, which usually has no primary outcome.
  • The abstract is in a language other than English or French.

This is a limit of rule-based extraction, not a flaw in the study. Check the full text for definitive information.

Multicenter status

A study is labeled multicentric when the abstract contains explicit keywords (multicenter, multicentre, multi-institutional) or a center count greater than one ("across 12 hospitals"). It is labeled monocentric only when the abstract explicitly describes a single-center study. Without such a mention, no label is given: the absence of a marker does not mean the study is monocentric.

Good to Know

MedLitSearch does not replace PubMed or a full systematic review method. It helps search, screen and structure the literature, and keeps every extracted value checkable against its source.
